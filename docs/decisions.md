# Decisions log

Every important decision, newest first. Colleagues read this before they work.

| Date | Decision | Decided by | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2026-11-16 | Build on FastAPI, Postgres, simple front ends and Docker Compose. Each organisation (Maryland, Virginia) gets its own Postgres database and database user, plus a shared identity database for logins. The chatbot runs in a credit-based Azure subscription with its spending limit on. Security events are logged as JSON lines. | Claude, to meet the course spec (founder to ratify) | Separate databases mean a wrong query cannot cross organisations, which keeps the never-mix rule simple to prove. Azure only offers a hard spending limit on credit-based subscriptions; pay-as-you-go budgets just alert. |
| 2026-11-16 | First security requirement (SR-1): a veteran's records and uploads are visible only to that veteran and their own assigned caseworker, and every upload is treated as untrusted input. Fictional veterans and fake documents only outside production. | Loic Djamen (founder) | Our users are vulnerable, caseworkers act on their behalf, and the documents (discharge papers, medical letters, income proof) are highly sensitive. Programme managers and grant reports get aggregates only. Tracked in Jira VAID-16. |
| 2026-11-16 | The chatbot runs on Azure AI Foundry, and Azure is VeteranAid's approved cloud for all data, with Entra ID for sign-in. | Claude (founder had no preference; founder to ratify) | One cloud keeps identity, data and the model inside one approved boundary. Many nonprofits already run Microsoft 365, so staff identities usually sit in Entra ID. |
| 2026-11-16 | Security framework is NIST CSF 2.0, with NIST AI RMF for the chatbot. | Claude (founder had no preference; founder to ratify) | CSF 2.0 covers the whole platform and is familiar to federal grant officers. The AI RMF covers the chatbot risks named in the risk appetite: no eligibility decisions, no training on veteran data. |
| 2026-11-16 | Keeping Maryland and Virginia apart comes before speed. No veteran data may cross between the two state programmes, or reach a partner who isn't entitled to it, through the portal, the API or the chatbot. Features that can't guarantee this wait, and caseworkers use manual workarounds in the meantime. | Loic Djamen (founder) | Cross-state exposure is our worst day: it would break the grant terms, lose veterans' trust and likely cost both grants at once. That's why the risk appetite trades slower delivery for stronger separation. |
